## Overall Composition
Low-angle cab view from the upper-left dock camera, looking slightly right and down across the front hood. The frame is dominated by two massive hyperbolic cooling towers rising from a concrete base just beyond the right shoulder of the interstate. The towers sit close enough that the nearer one fills roughly a third of the vertical frame; the second stands a short distance behind and to the right, partially occluded. Between and slightly behind them runs a dense electrical switchyard: lattice steel gantries, strings of porcelain and polymer insulators, and multiple horizontal bus bars that catch the late-afternoon light. A river is visible beyond the plant complex, but the primary subject is the industrial geometry.

## Cooling Towers
The nearer tower is a classic natural-draft hyperbolic shell, light gray concrete with faint vertical formwork lines still visible from casting. The base flares outward into a wide circular plinth; the waist narrows dramatically before the top opens again into a broad lip. Surface texture is matte with subtle staining and water marks that follow the curvature. The second tower mirrors the first in shape and color but reads smaller because of distance and slight haze. Both towers are dry at the lip—no visible plume—suggesting either low load or ambient conditions that do not force heavy evaporation. A thin safety railing and catwalk are just barely readable near the top of the nearer tower. The concrete has a slight warm cast from the low sun, turning the gray toward a muted taupe on the sunlit faces.

## Switchyard and Grid Infrastructure
Immediately to the right of the towers is a large outdoor switchyard. Tall lattice transmission towers (roughly 80–100 ft) stand in a loose grid. Multiple horizontal cross-arms carry bundles of conductors. Insulator strings hang in long vertical drops; some are glass (greenish tint) and some are modern polymer (brown-gray). Several high-voltage breakers and disconnect switches are visible as smaller metal structures at ground level. The ground inside the yard is pale gravel with narrow concrete equipment pads. A chain-link fence with barbed wire top runs along the near edge of the yard, roughly parallel to the highway. Warning signs are too small to read but their yellow-and-black shapes are clear. Overhead, several sets of transmission lines leave the yard and angle toward the horizon, sagging slightly between towers.

## Lighting and Atmosphere
Late-afternoon sun comes from the left-rear of the truck, throwing long soft shadows from the towers across the switchyard gravel. The light is warm but not golden-hour saturated—more of a pale amber. The sky is mostly clear with thin high cirrus. Atmospheric haze softens the far tower and the distant river line. No strong wind is evident; the insulator strings hang straight. Reflections on the cab hood and windshield are minimal because the camera is looking slightly downward.

## Foreground and Cab Context
